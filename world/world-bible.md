# World Bible

> Established facts come from Part 1. Anything marked _TBD_ is open for Chapter Two.

## The world in one paragraph

An isolated, walled village high in frozen mountains. Pre-industrial: barter economy, handmade goods, livestock, stone and timber houses, torches. Authority is religious — the priest's word decides what is holy and what is a witch. Beyond the gates: snow, mountains, and a valley behind the village used as a dumping ground for the unburiable.

## Era & technology level

Pre-industrial / medieval-equivalent. No firearms shown. Rope, blades, torches, farming, hand-crafts.

## ⭐ THE TRANSFORMATION RULE *(new sequel lore)*

> A rare old tale says **freely chosen protection, met by love that asks for no different
> body, may grant a dragon a new shape.**
>
> **It is a possibility, not a spell.**

### How it is introduced
The King shows Aurelia an old illustrated book in **SC03** and tells her the tale. He treats
it as a tale — *"It cannot be forced."* Nobody seeks it, nobody plans around it, and the
royal march in SC07 is caused by **the ransom**, not by any knowledge of a coming miracle.

### The required order of operations

```
1. freely chosen protection   →  Ember leaves the grave and rescues her   (SC08–09)
2. mercy                      →  "No more."  He spares the surrendering   (SC09)
3. love that asks for no
   different body             →  "I love you, Ember."  — said to a DRAGON (SC10)
4. the tear                   →  falls on his scales AFTER 1, 2 and 3     (SC10)
5. the change                 →  white-gold light, match cut              (SC10)
```

> ⚠️ **Never reorder these.** The tear *completes* an earned change; it is not a cure.

### Hard constraints

| Constraint | Reason |
|---|---|
| ❌ **Aurelia never knows** her love can transform him | She must not be courting a hidden prince |
| ❌ Never call the human body his **true** or morally superior form | Destroys the theme |
| ❌ The tear must not fall before the rescue **and** the mercy | Makes it a random cure |
| ❌ Elara cannot be resurrected | The core theme is grief **carried**, not undone |

> 📌 *This is new proposed sequel lore, not verified lore from Part 1.*

## History & mythology

### What the village believes

Fear is expressed in religious terms: *monster*, *witch*, *God will be pleased with me*. The village has a vocabulary for this already — suggesting prior lore about such creatures.

### The written record — NEW IN PART 2

The teaser states: ***"Now someone has written down what he really is."***

This establishes that somewhere there exists a **text** — a chronicle, bestiary, scripture or hunter's record — that names Ember's true nature. Implies:

- A literate institution beyond the village (church, order, scholar, archive)
- Ember's kind is **known** to someone
- Knowledge, not just force, is a weapon in Chapter Two

## Culture

- **Language:** English. Plain, simple, modern-accessible — not archaic.
- **Naming conventions:** Elara, Ember, Marta, Nessa, Elden, Ram — short, soft, vaguely Northern European.
- **Religion & ritual:** Priest-led. Burial rights are controlled and can be denied ("We cannot bury her in our graveyard").
- **Daily life:** Trade by barter, handmade crafts, meat from a butcher/market, livestock, cold.
