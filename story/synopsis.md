# Synopsis — THE MOTHER'S MONSTER: PART 2

> 28 minutes · 4 acts · 12 scenes · present tense

---

## Full synopsis

Months after the village killed Elara, her son has not moved. **Ember**, now immense, lies
curled around a human-sized grave on a ridge above a ruined village he burned in grief. He
keeps a vigil that no one asked him to keep. A paw rests beside the headstone; a tear falls.
He remembers his mother alive and gentle — and he remembers what he did in anger. *"Mom...
I'm still here."*

**Aurelia**, a princess of the winter valley kingdom, walks the pine trail with two escorts
and finds him. She is frightened, but she sees mourning, not menace. She does not approach
without permission and she does not demand his trust. *"I won't come closer. I only wanted
you to know you're not alone."*

At the castle she asks her father not to hunt him. The **King** does not pretend the burned
village didn't happen; he names the cost and his duty. Aurelia doesn't deny it — *"Then let
him choose something better."* He permits guarded visits. He also shows her an old
illustrated book and an old tale: that freely chosen protection, met by love that asks for
no different body, can change a dragon's shape. He is careful to call it a tale, not a plan.
*"It cannot be forced."* Aurelia answers: *"I am not trying to change him."*

She returns three times, and each visit does different work. The first, she offers food and
accepts his refusal. The second, she sits apart and clears snow from the grave; a bird call
turns his head — his hearing is extraordinary. The third, she simply comes back, wanting
nothing, and he finally eats a little and watches her go. *"I can't replace your mother.
But you don't have to be alone."*

Her kindness has a route, and the route has a witness. A bandit **scout** has been watching.
At the quarry camp across the ravine, the **leader** sees a princess worth a ransom and a
dragon who will never leave his dead. *"A grieving beast won't leave his dead. The King will
pay for his living daughter."* The **enforcer** sets rope and drags a fallen log across the
trail.

The ambush comes on her next walk. The escorts are separated, stunned and pinned — not
casually killed. Aurelia fights and leaves a deliberate trace at the split boulder. They take
her to the camp in the late afternoon, where she tests her knots and watches the gate, looking
for a way out rather than waiting to be found. The ransom goes to the King before nightfall.
He is terrified and he organises anyway: the **Captain** takes a fast vanguard on foot, the
King follows with the knights on the harder snowy route. *"Bring my daughter home. Take
prisoners if you can."*

That night Aurelia works a binding loose and is caught; the enforcer re-ties her. And she
screams — *"Help me, Ember!"* The sound runs down the ravine and reaches the ridge. He hears
her. He rises, and he is torn: the promise is here, the voice is there.

**Elara's spirit** appears once, beside her own grave. She has not come back to life and she
will not. She has come to let him go. *"I cannot return, my son. But you do not lose me by
loving someone else."* He protests — *"I promised I would stay."* She answers: *"That
princess truly loves you. Go save her. It is my final wish."* She reminds him, as she always
did, to be merciful. She touches his lowered head. He kisses the headstone and flies. The
spirit fades. Nothing about her death is undone.

At the camp, anger offers him the old answer: burn everything. He refuses it. He puts himself
between the bandits and Aurelia, shields her, smashes the weapon rack and destroys the camp's
ability to fight — and the moment a man drops his blade, Ember stops. The Captain's vanguard
arrives at the rear gate and sees a dragon protecting human lives, including lives he could
have taken. *"Hold your weapons. He's protecting her."* The leader is taken for royal justice.
Aurelia is freed outright. *"No more."*

She reaches him while he is still a dragon. She takes his lowered face in her arms and says
*"I love you, Ember."* Only then — after the choice, after the mercy — does her tear fall on
his scales. White-gold light. The shape changes. The same eyes look back at her out of a new
face. Fear, exhaustion and magical shock take her and she faints; he catches her. The Captain
secures the perimeter and helps them to the river bank.

At dawn, human Ember is tending her by the river when she wakes and knows him — by his eyes
and his voice, not his beauty. The King arrives. The Captain testifies to what he saw. Ember
makes no claim that one night cancels a burned village: *"I cannot undo what I did. I can
choose what I do next."* The King concedes the thing he got wrong — *"I judged your shape. I
should have watched your deeds."* — and then asks the only question that matters: *"Is this
your choice, Aurelia?"* *"Yes. I loved him before any of this."*

They marry in the castle courtyard, red roses in the garlands, no spectacle. And afterwards
Ember climbs back to the ridge and lays fresh red roses on his mother's grave, Aurelia beside
him — not in Elara's place. A second visit tells us this is a ritual, not a farewell. Above
them, in the clouds, Elara's face appears, smiling. Not resurrection. Memory, carried forward.

*"I still carry you, Mom."*

## One paragraph

A grieving dragon who has not left his mother's grave is befriended by a princess who asks
nothing of him. When bandits take her for ransom and her cry reaches him across the ravine,
his mother's spirit appears once — not to return, but to release him. He destroys the camp's
weapons without killing its men, and when the last enemy surrenders, he stops. The princess
embraces his dragon face and tells him she loves him; her tear completes a change the old
tales said was possible but could never be forced. He wakes human, is judged by his deeds
rather than his shape, and marries her — and still brings red roses to the grave.
