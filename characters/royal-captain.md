## One line

The soldier who arrives in time to see what the dragon chose *not* to do.

## Why he exists

Without the Captain, SC11 is unearned. He is the mechanism that converts Ember's private mercy into **public evidence** — the King changes his mind because a trusted officer saw it, not because the plot needs him to (principle 7).

He also solves three production problems at once:

| Problem | The Captain solves it |
|---|---|
| How does the King learn what happened? | He testifies |
| How is the bandit leader taken alive for "royal justice"? | He secures him |
| How do an exhausted princess and a newly human man get to the river? | He escorts them |

## Function by scene

| Scene | Function |
|---|---|
| SC07 | Given the vanguard. Leads **on foot**, faster than the main royal group on the harder snowy route |
| SC09 | Arrives at the **rear-right gate**. Witnesses Ember shielding people he could have killed. Calls off his own men. Secures the leader |
| SC10 | Secures the perimeter. Helps Aurelia and human Ember reach the river bank |
| SC11 | **Corroborates the rescue and the restraint** to the King |

## Personality

Disciplined, observant, unflappable. The kind of officer who stops his men mid-charge because he is actually looking. Loyal to the King *and* honest about what he saw — the two never conflict, which is the point.

## Voice

Clipped, operational. Commands, not opinions. His one great line is an order, not a speech.

## Signature line

- *"Hold your weapons. He's protecting her."* (SC09)

## Staging note

His **arrival must be set up in SC07**, not discovered in SC09 — the whole no-coincidence design depends on the audience already knowing the vanguard is on the trail.
